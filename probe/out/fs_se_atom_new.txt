		<summary type="text">The complete short fiction of Joseph Conrad.</summary>
		<content type="html">&lt;p&gt;&lt;a href="https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/joseph-conrad"&gt;Joseph Conrad&lt;/a&gt;, the Polish-born writer who published exclusively in English, is known as much for his short fiction as for his novels. Almost all of his stories were first serialized in periodicals such as &lt;i&gt;The Pall Mall Magazine&lt;/i&gt;, &lt;i&gt;Harper’s Magazine&lt;/i&gt;, and &lt;i&gt;Blackwood’s Magazine&lt;/i&gt;. They were later republished in seven collections: &lt;i&gt;Tales of Unrest&lt;/i&gt;, &lt;i&gt;Youth: A Narrative and Two Other Stories&lt;/i&gt;, &lt;i&gt;Typhoon and Other Stories&lt;/i&gt;, &lt;i&gt;A Set of Six&lt;/i&gt;, &lt;i&gt;’Twixt Land and Sea&lt;/i&gt;, &lt;i&gt;Within the Tides&lt;/i&gt;, and &lt;i&gt;Tales of Hearsay&lt;/i&gt;. Many of his stories deal with the sea and exotic locales (the Malay Archipelago, Central Africa, South America), while others reflect his wider interests, such as his fascination with the Napoleonic era.&lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt;Conrad’s most famous novella contained in this collection, “Heart of Darkness,” was originally published serially as a three-part story. It is a short but thematically complex novella exploring colonialism, humanity, and what constitutes a savage society. Set in the Congo in Central Africa, the tale is told in the frame of the recollections of one Charles Marlow, a captain of an ivory steamer. Marlow’s search for the mysterious and powerful “first-class agent” Kurtz gives way to a nuanced and powerful commentary on the horrors of colonialism, called by some the most analyzed work at university-level instruction.&lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt;Other notable works include “An Outpost of Progress,” a precursor to “Heart of Darkness,” “Youth,” based on Conrad’s own maritime experiences, “Typhoon,” in which a stubborn captain takes on a force of nature, “The Duel,” later filmed by Ridley Scott as “The Duellists,” and “The Secret Sharer,” in which a captain harbors a fugitive. This collection comprises all the short stories and novellas from Conrad’s seven short fiction collections, plus “The Sisters,” an unfinished short story published posthumously. They’re arranged in order of first publication, which in most cases means their magazine serialization.&lt;/p&gt;</content>

		<content type="html">&lt;p&gt;Theo Trist and Brenda Gilholme have known one another since childhood. He was Nero’s tutor, and then his advisor when Nero became emperor in 54 CE. After Seneca retired from public life, he wrote a series of letters to his friend Lucilius, the procurator of Sicily. These letters were circulated right before Seneca’s death in 65 CE, when Nero forced him to commit suicide for his alleged involvement in a conspiracy.&lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt;These letters, which were written for widespread publication as well as directly to Lucilius, mainly focus on guiding the reader to a life well-lived. At times, the advice is regarding specific personal issues, such as when he gives a review of a book written by Lucilius. Other letters give Seneca’s thoughts on broader issues like slavery, independence of thought, and virtue. Through all of them, his Stoicism shines through, whether in his metaphysics (like when he writes on “the god within us”) or in his focus on how virtue is the only good. His diverse interests are also on display, as when he addresses current events (like the burning of Lyons), or when he speculates on science (like when he offers his thoughts on animal instincts).&lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt;These letters have been popular and influential since their initial circulation, and were widely read from the Roman period, up through the Middle Ages, and into the Renaissance, where &lt;a href="https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/michel-de-montaigne"&gt;Michel de Montaigne&lt;/a&gt; used them as a model for his &lt;a href="https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/michel-de-montaigne/essays/charles-cotton"&gt;&lt;i&gt;Essays&lt;/i&gt;&lt;/a&gt;. They remain highly regarded today for their practical and sage advice.&lt;/p&gt;</content>
